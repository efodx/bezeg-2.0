# Bézier study chapter: source notes

These notes audit current repository behavior for a proposed standalone Bézier study chapter. They report source behavior, not browser-tested usability. Routes below use the catalog's `/bezeg-2.0` base path.

## Existing demonstrations and prepared examples

- The ordinary polynomial Bézier demonstration is catalogued as `bezier` at `/bezeg-2.0/bezier/curve`; its graph default is the cubic control polygon \((-4,-3),(-3,2),(2,2),(3,-2)\). The current guided lesson evaluates this curve at \(t=1/2\) as \((-1/2,7/8)\). This value checks directly from cubic Bernstein weights \((1/8,3/8,3/8,1/8)\): weighted coordinate sums are \((-4-9+6+3)/8=-1/2\) and \((-3+6+6-2)/8=7/8\). See [catalog definitions](../../src/demonstrations.ts), [ordinary graph default](../../src/graphs/curves/bezier/BezierCurveGraph.tsx), and [guided Bézier content](../../src/learning/content/guidedLessons.ts).
- De Casteljau's `decasteljau` route is `/bezeg-2.0/bezier/decasteljau`. Its default has five points \((-4,-3),(-3,2),(0,3),(3,2),(4,-3)\), hence degree four, and shows the construction initially at \(t=1/2\). The second route `decasteljau-alternative` (`/bezeg-2.0/bezier/decasteljau2`) uses the same five points but starts with its construction hidden. The quartic midpoint is \((0,7/4)\), also recorded in the guided example and independently checked from weights \((1,4,6,4,1)/16\). See [De Casteljau graph](../../src/graphs/curves/bezier/DecasteljauGraph.tsx), [alternative graph](../../src/graphs/curves/bezier/DecasteljauGraph2.tsx), [guided De Casteljau content](../../src/learning/content/guidedLessons.ts), and the [polynomial curve evaluator and full construction](../../src/bezeg/impl/curve/polynomial-bezier-curve.ts).
- Therefore, a chapter that sends a learner from the ordinary cubic example to the current De Casteljau route silently changes degree and control points. A shared worked example should explicitly change the graph/example, or the chapter should explain the distinction; do not imply the quartic's midpoint belongs to the ordinary cubic default.

## What learners can currently investigate

- **Control points and shape:** The ordinary Bézier graph uses editable JSXGraph control points backed by `PolynomialBezierCurve`. Learners can drag them, and the shared curve object can show or hide its control polygon and convex hull. The guided graph-action layer can prepare polygon and hull visibility. The current ordinary graph lesson also supports displaying an evaluation point for a selected parameter. See [curve adapter](../../src/graphs/curves/object/JXGBezierCurve.ts), [point-controlled curve](../../src/graphs/curves/object/AbstractJXGPointControlledCurve.ts), [shared lesson actions](../../src/graphs/base/lesson/curveLessonActions.ts), and [guided action declarations](../../src/learning/content/guidedLessons.ts).
- **De Casteljau construction:** The polynomial model computes the full sequence of rows, and `DecasteljauGraph` displays the construction. Its parameter control varies \(t\), with play, pause, stop, and replay controls; it can also show the full curve alongside the partial curve ending at the evaluated point. This makes successive linear interpolations, the changing intermediate points, and the final point's position on the curve inspectable. See [model recurrence](../../src/bezeg/impl/curve/polynomial-bezier-curve.ts) and [graph controls/scene](../../src/graphs/curves/bezier/DecasteljauGraph.tsx).
- **Bernstein basis:** The separate `bernstein` demonstration at `/bezeg-2.0/bernstein` starts at degree 1 (two basis functions) and supports degrees 0 through 5. It draws the basis functions and can switch to a stacked-area view. The current foundation lesson prepares degree 5 and the stacked view. This can support reading basis shape, counting \(n+1\) functions, and seeing the sum-to-one picture across \([0,1]\). It is a separate graph: there is no linked parameter cursor or automatic connection to a simultaneously displayed Bézier curve. The plot is visual evidence for selected degrees, not proof of the general binomial identity. See [Bernstein graph](../../src/graphs/bernstein/BernsteinGraph.tsx), [foundation lesson](../../src/learning/content/foundationGuidedLessons.ts), and [catalog definitions](../../src/demonstrations.ts).
- **Affine operations:** The `affine` route (`/bezeg-2.0/bezier/affine`) starts from another cubic example and provides translation, scale, rotation, and reflection controls that transform the curve's control points. This can illustrate how the whole curve follows these operations. It is not currently a side-by-side before/after comparison, a general affine-map editor, or a demonstration that arbitrary nonlinear maps commute with Bézier evaluation. See [affine graph](../../src/graphs/curves/bezier/AffineTransformsBezierCurveGraph.tsx) and [catalog definitions](../../src/demonstrations.ts).

## Mathematical claims to keep precise

- A representation with \(n+1\) control points has nominal degree \(n\), while special control-point configurations can give it a lower actual polynomial degree. The evaluator uses the De Casteljau recurrence and can retain its full rows for drawing. Bernstein form is explained in the guided lesson, and the basis graph separately plots its terms. The graph implementations do not on their own establish equivalence for every degree and input.
- Endpoint interpolation and the convex-hull property are stated in the current lesson. The shared hull display can help inspect the latter. Its guarantee is for \(0\le t\le1\), because Bernstein weights are nonnegative there and sum to one; do not generalize this convex-combination argument to extrapolation parameters outside that interval.
- **Control-point influence is global in parameter, not local support.** For an ordinary polynomial Bézier curve, moving \(P_i\) by vector \(v\) changes \(B(t)\) by \(B_i^n(t)v\). For an interior control point, \(B_i^n(t)>0\) at every \(0<t<1\); its displacement therefore affects the entire open curve parameter interval, with magnitude varying by \(t\), while endpoint values stay fixed. The existing guided lesson's phrase “local influence” in `bezier-influence` should not be carried into the chapter as a precise mathematical claim. It may describe a visibly stronger region, but that observation needs qualification.
- The hull and basis visualizations are finite examples and rendered samples. State general properties mathematically, distinguish them from what a learner observes, and explain why the algebra supports the general statement. In particular, a plotted curve staying inside a shown hull is not itself proof for all \(t\).

## Scope limits and verification

- Current graphs already support the proposed chapter's core explorations without a new geometric construction: editable control points and polygon/hull visibility, the full De Casteljau rows with a variable parameter, Bernstein basis and stacked views, and selected affine operations.
- The current material does **not** establish a synchronized chapter activity spanning multiple demonstrations, a persistent chapter return position, a linked Bernstein weight cursor, arbitrary affine-map controls, or a browser-verified chapter experience. Those are product/runtime design decisions, not existing graph capabilities.
- Source inspection and the two midpoint calculations above are the verification performed for these notes. No browser interaction or application test suite was run; no claim of classroom readiness or visual verification is made.
